# Decision Brief: Additional Engineering Hire

---

## Decision Statement

Should we approve hiring an additional Senior Software Engineer to address current workload and delivery timeline concerns?

---

## Context

**Current Situation:**
The engineering team is currently comprised of 4 developers working on multiple concurrent projects. Over the past quarter, we have missed two key delivery deadlines and team velocity has decreased by approximately 30%.

**Problem/Opportunity:**
The team is operating at capacity with increasing technical debt and feature requests. Current workload is stretching resources thin, resulting in:
- Missed delivery timelines
- Increased bug rate due to rushed work
- Team burnout indicators
- Inability to address technical debt

**Urgency:**
Two major client deliverables are scheduled for Q2 (8 weeks away). Current velocity suggests we will miss at least one of these deadlines without intervention.

---

## Options Under Consideration

### Option A: Hire Senior Software Engineer

**Description:**
Add a Senior Software Engineer to the team through standard hiring process.

**Effort:**
- Recruiting: 4-6 weeks
- Onboarding: 2-3 weeks
- Full productivity: 8-10 weeks total

**Investment:**
- Salary: $140,000-$160,000 annually
- Benefits & overhead: ~30% = $42,000-$48,000
- Recruiting costs: ~$15,000
- First-year total: ~$200,000-$220,000

**Risk:**
- Hiring timeline may extend beyond Q2 deadlines
- Onboarding temporarily reduces existing team velocity
- Cultural fit uncertainty

**Expected Outcome:**
- Long-term capacity increase of 20-25%
- Improved delivery reliability after ramp-up period
- Ability to address technical debt
- Reduced team burnout

---

### Option B: Engage Contractor/Fractional Developer

**Description:**
Hire an experienced contractor or fractional developer for 6-month engagement.

**Effort:**
- Vendor selection: 1 week
- Onboarding: 1 week
- Immediate productivity on well-defined tasks

**Investment:**
- Hourly rate: $100-$150/hour
- Full-time equivalent: ~$200,000-$240,000 for 6 months
- No long-term commitment

**Risk:**
- Higher hourly cost than employee
- Limited institutional knowledge transfer
- Contractor may not be available for Q3/Q4 work
- Potential integration challenges

**Expected Outcome:**
- Immediate capacity increase for Q2
- Flexible engagement - can extend or end as needed
- Specialized expertise for specific challenges
- No long-term obligation if workload decreases

---

### Option C: Redistribute & Prioritize (No Hire)

**Description:**
Re-scope current projects, defer non-essential features, and optimize existing team workflow.

**Effort:**
- Immediate: Re-prioritization meetings
- Ongoing: Strict scope management
- Process improvements: Sprint optimization

**Investment:**
- No additional headcount cost
- Opportunity cost: Deferred features and improvements
- Potential client relationship strain from delays

**Risk:**
- High risk of missing Q2 deadlines
- Team burnout likely to continue
- Technical debt will accumulate
- Potential loss of client trust

**Expected Outcome:**
- Maintain current team structure
- Deliver reduced scope
- Preserve budget
- High stress and quality risk

---

**Scoring Guide:** 1=Poor, 3=Average, 5=Excellent

---

## Recommendation

**Selected: Option A - Hire Senior Software Engineer**

**Confidence:** Medium-High

**Rationale:**

1. **Balances immediate and long-term needs:** While Option B (Contractor) provides faster relief for Q2, Option A builds sustainable capacity for the remainder of the year and beyond.

2. **Most favorable overall score:** Highest weighted score across all criteria, particularly strong on long-term capacity (most important criterion at 25% weight).

3. **Team sustainability:** Addresses burnout concerns and technical debt, not just short-term delivery.

4. **Cost-effective over time:** While first-year cost is ~$200K, annualized cost in years 2+ drops to ~$185K with full productivity, compared to contractor's recurring $200K+ cost.

**Key Risk - Timeline:**
The primary concern is the 8-10 week timeline to full productivity, which may not fully address Q2 deadlines.

**Mitigation Plan:**
- Begin hiring process immediately
- Engage freelancer for 4-6 week bridge during onboarding period
- Re-prioritize Q2 deliverables to focus on highest-impact items
- Provide clear scope documentation for new hire's rapid onboarding
