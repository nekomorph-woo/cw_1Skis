# Stakeholder Analysis: Engineering Hire Decision

---

## Key Stakeholders

### Stakeholder 1: Engineering Manager / CTO

**Role:** Decision Maker

**Power Level:** High - Has final approval authority

**Interest Level:** High - Directly affects team performance and budget

**Current Position:** Unknown (decision pending)

**Decision Criteria:**
- Budget availability and allocation
- Long-term team strategy
- Q2 delivery confidence
- ROI on headcount investment

**Communication Preferences:**
- Format: Decision brief with data
- Detail level: Executive summary + detailed options
- Timing: Before budget planning cycle

**Key Message:**
"Hiring now prevents missed Q2 deliveries and builds sustainable capacity. The investment pays for itself through reduced technical debt and improved delivery velocity."

**Potential Objections:**
- **"Budget is tight"**
  - Response: "Compare hiring cost to cost of missed deadlines. One missed client delivery costs $50K+ in penalties and relationship damage. Hiring costs ~$200K/year or ~$17K/month. Preventing 1-2 missed deliveries pays for 3-4 months of salary."

- **"Timeline is too long for Q2"**
  - Response: "Valid concern. I recommend engaging a contractor for 4-6 weeks as a bridge. The permanent hire addresses long-term capacity while the contractor addresses Q2."

- **"What if workload decreases?"**
  - Response: "Our roadmap shows sustained demand through Q4. Also, we've accumulated technical debt that requires attention. Even with stable workload, there's 6+ months of catch-up work."

---

### Stakeholder 2: CEO / Business Owner

**Role:** Influencer (often consulted on budget decisions)

**Power Level:** High - Can influence or veto hiring decisions

**Interest Level:** Medium - Cares about client satisfaction and margins

**Current Position:** Likely supportive if client delivery risk is addressed

**Decision Criteria:**
- Client satisfaction and retention
- Revenue impact
- Profitability
- Strategic growth

**Communication Preferences:**
- Format: Business impact summary
- Detail level: High-level outcomes, minimal technical detail
- Timing: As part of business updates

**Key Message:**
"Current team capacity risks client deliverables. Hiring prevents delivery delays, protects client relationships, and supports growth. The investment strengthens our delivery capability."

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Client delivery delays in Q1: [Specific examples if available]
- Team velocity decrease: 30% over past quarter
- Client relationship risk: High-value deliverables at risk

**Potential Objections:**
- **"Can we optimize instead?"**
  - Response: "We've optimized sprint process and improved prioritization. These improvements helped but aren't sufficient given current demand. We're at the limit of what efficiency gains can provide."

- **"What's the revenue impact of hiring?"**
  - Response: "Hiring costs ~$200K/year. Increased capacity allows us to take on [X] more projects or reduce outsourced development costs of ~$150K/year. Net impact is approximately neutral in year 1, positive in year 2."

---

### Stakeholder 3: Current Engineering Team

**Role:** Implementers (affected by decision)

**Power Level:** Low - No formal decision power but high influence on success

**Interest Level:** High - Directly affects their workload and team dynamics

**Current Position:** Likely supportive (overwhelmed team)

**Decision Criteria:**
- Work-life balance
- Quality of work
- Team collaboration
- Career growth opportunities

**Communication Preferences:**
- Format: Team announcement with Q&A
- Detail level: Transparent about process and timeline
- Timing: After decision is made but before external announcement

**Key Message:**
"We're adding capacity to the team to address current workload and improve work-life balance. We're looking for a senior team member who can contribute quickly and mentor others."

**Engagement Considerations:**
- Involve team in interview process
- Be transparent about timeline (8+ weeks to full productivity)
- Set expectations that workload won't immediately improve
- Address concerns about team dynamics and culture

**Potential Concerns:**
- **"Will this change our compensation or roles?"**
  - Response: "No changes to current roles. The new hire adds capacity, doesn't replace anyone. In fact, additional headcount should reduce pressure on everyone."

- **"What if the new person isn't a good fit?"**
  - Response: "We'll involve the team in the interview process. Standard probation period applies. We're prioritizing cultural fit and technical capability."

---

### Stakeholder 4: Finance / Budget Owner

**Role:** Influencer (controls budget allocation)

**Power Level:** Medium - Can approve or block budget

**Interest Level:** Medium - Cares about budget adherence and forecasting

**Current Position:** Unknown (depends on budget availability)

**Decision Criteria:**
- Budget availability
- Adherence to financial plan
- Forecast accuracy

**Communication Preferences:**
- Format: Budget request with justification
- Detail level: Line-item costs, timing, and ROI analysis
- Timing: During budget planning or with lead time

**Key Message:**
"Requesting $220K for FY2025 headcount: Senior Software Engineer. Investment prevents client delivery risk (potential $100K+ penalties) and supports revenue growth. ROI positive within 12 months."

**Budget Details:**
- Q1 2025: $15K (recruiting)
- Q2-Q4 2025: $185K (salary + benefits)
- Total FY2025: ~$200K

**ROI Calculation:**
- Cost avoidance: 1-2 missed deliveries @ $50K each = $50-100K
- Revenue enablement: Additional projects @ $100K margin = $100K
- Outsourced work brought in-house: $150K savings
- Net impact: Positive within first year

**Potential Objections:**
- **"This wasn't in the budget"**
  - Response: "Understood. This is an unforeseen need due to increased demand. Can we reallocate from [other budget category] or treat as Q1 adjustment given business need?"

## Influence Strategy

### Primary Target: Engineering Manager / CTO

**Approach:**
1. Schedule decision review meeting
2. Present decision brief with options analysis
3. Address timeline concern with contractor bridge proposal
4. Emphasize long-term capacity benefits
5. Discuss budget approval process

**Success Criteria:**
- Understands the decision context
- Agrees with the recommendation
- Approves budget allocation
- Supports hiring process

---

### Secondary Target: CEO

**Approach:**
1. Brief conversation or email after manager approval
2. Focus on business impact and client relationships
3. Keep technical details minimal
4. Emphasize growth enablement

**Success Criteria:**
- Awareness of hiring decision
- Supportive of investment
- No objections

---

## Risk Mitigation

### Risk: Decision delayed beyond Q2 relevance

**Mitigation:**
- Present decision with urgency (Q2 deadlines 8 weeks away)
- Offer contractor bridge as immediate action
- Begin pre-approval activities (job description drafting)

### Risk: Budget not available

**Mitigation:**
- Explore reallocation from other categories
- Consider alternative start dates (Q2 vs Q1)
- Present ROI to justify budget adjustment

### Risk: Team concerns about hiring process

**Mitigation:**
- Involve team in interviews
- Be transparent about timeline and expectations
- Address cultural fit explicitly in job description

---

## Follow-Up Plan

### If Approved:
- Communicate decision to all stakeholders
- Initiate hiring process immediately
- Schedule team announcement meeting
- Begin contractor search if bridge approved

### If Rejected:
- Reassess Q2 capacity and timeline
- Communicate realistic delivery expectations
- Implement Option C (redistribute and prioritize)
- Re-propose hire in future when appropriate
