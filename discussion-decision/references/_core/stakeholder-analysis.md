## Stakeholder Identification

### Primary Stakeholder Categories

| Category | Characteristics | Questions |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Decision Makers** | Authority to approve/reject | What do they need to say yes? |
| **Influencers** | Shape decisions without authority | Who do decision makers listen to? |
| **Implementers** | Execute the decision | What do they need to succeed? |
| **Affected Parties** | Impacted by outcomes | How will they be impacted? |

## Common Stakeholder Archetypes

### The Skeptic
- **Traits**: Questions everything, demands data
- **Strategy**: Come prepared with evidence, acknowledge concerns
- **Don't**: Get defensive, dismiss questions
- **Do**: Use their critical thinking to strengthen your case

### The Visionary
- **Traits**: Focuses on future, impatient with details
- **Strategy**: Paint the big picture, minimize process
- **Don't**: Get bogged down in implementation details
- **Do**: Connect to strategic outcomes

### The Pragmatist
- **Traits**: Risk-averse, practical, detail-oriented
- **Strategy**: Show realistic plans, address risks head-on
- **Don't**: Overpromise, gloss over challenges
- **Do**: Provide concrete steps and mitigation

### The Time-Constrained
- **Traits**: Busy, wants bottom line, distracted
- **Strategy**: Executive summary first, offer follow-up
- **Don't**: Long emails/meetings without clear asks
- **Do**: One-page briefs, clear subject lines

### The Relationship-Focused
- **Traits**: Values trust, harmony, personal connection
- **Strategy**: Build rapport, acknowledge others' contributions
- **Don't**: Blindside, go around them
- **Do**: Keep them informed, involve early

## Influence Mapping

### Identify the Real Decision Maker

Sometimes the person with the title isn't the one who decides:

**Questions to Ask:**
- Who said yes to similar decisions?
- Whose opinion carries the most weight?
- Who has veto power?
- Who needs to feel heard for agreement?

**Assessment Methods:**
- Review past decisions: Who actually decided?
- Ask informally: "Who typically signs off on this?"
- Observe meetings: Who do others look to?

### Map the Influence Network

```
           [Decision Maker]
                  |
      +-----------+-----------+
      |                       |
 [Influencer 1]         [Influencer 2]
      |                       |
  [Group A]              [Group B]
```

**Key Questions:**
- Who influences the influencers?
- Where are the consensus gaps?
- Who can bridge opposing views?
- Who needs to be won over first?

## Pre-Meeting Stakeholder Prep

### Research Checklist
- [ ] Review their recent communications/emails
- [ ] Check their past decisions on similar issues
- [ ] Understand their current priorities
- [ ] Identify their known concerns or objections
- [ ] Find common ground or shared interests

### Meeting Strategy
- [ ] Define clear outcome for this meeting
- [ ] Prepare 3 key messages
- [ ] Anticipate 3 likely objections
- [ ] Bring relevant data/evidence
- [ ] Plan follow-up actions

### During the Meeting
- [ ] Start by confirming their priorities
- [ ] Listen more than you talk (first 10 minutes)
- [ ] Acknowledge their perspective
- [ ] Connect your ask to their goals
- [ ] Get specific commitment before leaving

## Stakeholder Alignment Techniques

### The "Pre-Wire"
- Meet with key stakeholders before group meetings
- Get their input and incorporate feedback
- Build consensus one-on-one first
- Reduces surprises and public disagreements

### The Champion Approach
- Identify and empower enthusiastic supporters
- Give them information and talking points
- Ask them to influence their peers
- Let them advocate from within their group

### The Trial Balloon
- Float ideas informally to gauge reaction
- Test specific elements of a proposal
- Gather feedback without formal commitment
- Refine approach before formal presentation

### The Devil's Advocate
- Assign someone to argue against your proposal
- Identifies weaknesses before stakeholders do
- Strengthens the final proposal
- Shows you've considered alternatives
