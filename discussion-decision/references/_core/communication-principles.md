# Communication Principles

Universal guidelines for effective communication in technical and business contexts.

---

## The Pyramid Principle

Structure communication for maximum clarity and impact:

```
        [Conclusion]
           /    \
      [Support]  [Support]
        /  \        /  \
   [Detail] [Detail] [Detail] [Detail]
```

**Rule:** Lead with the conclusion, then provide supporting arguments.

**Why:** Respects audience's time, enables quick understanding, allows for efficient drilling down.

---

## The 7 Cs of Communication

1. **Clear** - Message is unambiguous
2. **Concise** - No unnecessary words
3. **Concrete** - Specific, not vague
4. **Correct** - Accurate information
5. **Coherent** - Logical flow
6. **Complete** - All necessary information
7. **Courteous** - Respectful tone

## Audience Analysis Matrix

| Audience Type | Detail Level | Format Preference | Decision Style |
|---------------|--------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Executive | High-level summary | Bullet points, visuals | Quick, data-driven |
| Technical | In-depth analysis | Specifications, diagrams | Thorough, methodical |
| Operational | Action-focused | Checklists, timelines | Practical, immediate |
| Client | Value-oriented | Benefits, outcomes | Relationship-based |

## Message Structure Formula

```
[Subject Line] - Action required + Topic + Deadline

Opening: State the purpose and what you need
Context: Why this matters now
Details: Supporting information (3 key points max)
Ask: Clear action request with deadline
Contact: How to reach you with questions
```

## Email Best Practices

### Subject Lines
- Bad: "Update"
- Good: "ACTION REQUIRED: Q1 Budget Approval - Due Friday 5pm"

### Opening Lines
- Bad: "I hope this email finds you well"
- Good: "I'm writing to request your approval on..."

### Action Requests
- Bad: "Let me know what you think"
- Good: "Please confirm by Thursday if we should proceed with Option A"

### Closing Lines
- Bad: "Best regards,"
- Good: "I'm available to discuss on Wednesday if you have questions"

## Meeting Communication Rules

### Before the Meeting
- Send agenda 24h in advance
- Specify decision points (not just discussion topics)
- Include prep materials with time estimates

### During the Meeting
- Start with purpose and expected outcome
- Use parking lot for off-topic items
- Summarize decisions and action items before ending

### After the Meeting
- Send summary within 24h
- Clearly document: Decisions, Actions, Owners, Due dates
- Include "by when" for each action item

## Difficult Conversation Framework

### The DESC Script
- **D**escribe - State the facts objectively
- **E**xpress - Share your feelings/thoughts
- **S**pecify - Clearly state what you need
- **C**onsequences - Explain positive outcome of agreement

### Example
```
[D] The project deadline was missed by two weeks.
[E] I'm concerned about client trust and team morale.
[S] I need a communication plan for the client and a revised timeline.
[C] This will maintain our reputation and give the team realistic targets.
```

## Question Formulation

### Open Questions (Exploration)
- "What are your thoughts on...?"
- "How do you see this playing out?"
- "What concerns do you have?"

### Closed Questions (Confirmation)
- "Do we have agreement on...?"
- "Can we proceed with Option A?"
- "Is the deadline achievable?"

### Probing Questions (Detail)
- "What makes you say that?"
- "Can you help me understand...?"
- "What would need to be true for...?"
